Same situation

Your teammate costs your team the win. What do you do next?

The match is close. A teammate makes an error and it costs you the game. You feel frustrated, disappointed and flat. Your first feeling is normal. Your next response matters.

Tap an athlete to see how it plays out.

  • Points the finger straight away.
  • Complains to others after the match.
  • Makes the teammate feel worse.
  • Brings tension into the group.

Likely outcome: short term release, less trust, weaker team culture.

  • Takes a moment to cool off.
  • Checks in with the teammate.
  • Talks about what the team can learn.
  • Keeps standards high without attacking the person.

Likely outcome: more trust, better learning, stronger team culture.

For parents

Your support shapes the athlete.

You don't need to be a coach. A few small habits at home make a big difference.

Protect the routine

Screens off, calm evenings and enough sleep. Most young athletes do better on around 8 to 10 hours.

Praise the effort

Results come and go. Notice the work, the attitude and how they treat teammates.

Talk after, not during

Let the feelings settle first. The best conversations happen on the drive home, not on the sideline.
