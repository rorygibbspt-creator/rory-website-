# Filming it at home in Clapham

You don't need a studio or a green screen. In the main talk you're a 300 pixel circle in the corner, so what matters is
your face, your voice and the light. The intro and outro are full frame, so they need one corner of the flat that looks
good and can be set up the same way every time, because you'll film a new intro for every school.

## The home set

Pick one corner and keep it as "the set".

- **Face the window.** Soft daylight on your face is the best light you'll get. Never sit with the window behind you.
- **Background:** a plain wall or a tidy shelf about two metres behind you. Put the book, the Athlete Deck box, a plant
  and one rowing thing on the shelf (a framed photo, or the medal in its case). Nothing else.
- **What to wear:** the navy hoodie. It matches your illustrated character on the website and the slides, so the real you and
  the drawn you look like the same brand. Same top for every recording so intros match the main talk.
- **Mark it.** Tape on the floor for the tripod legs and the chair. Take a phone photo of the framing. Then every school's
  intro looks exactly like the last one.
- **Sit slightly left of centre,** chest up, eyes about a third of the way down the frame. That leaves room on the left
  for the name strip and on the right for the school's crest.

## Kit

In order of how much difference it makes:

1. **A clip on wireless mic** (DJI Mic, Rode Wireless GO or similar). Bad sound loses people faster than a bad picture.
2. **A phone tripod** at eye height. Use the back camera, not the selfie camera.
3. **A teleprompter.** A phone teleprompter app is fine to start. A small beam splitter teleprompter that clips over the
   phone lets you read while looking straight down the lens, which is what makes it feel like you're talking to the parent.
4. **One soft LED light** with a diffuser, for evenings and grey days. Point it at your face from beside the camera.

Camera settings: landscape, 4K if the phone allows, 25 frames a second, focus and exposure locked (press and hold on your face).
Filming in 4K means the same footage can be cropped into the circle or used full frame.

## Sound in a flat

- Rug on the floor, curtains closed (not over the window you're using for light), sofa cushions out of shot behind the camera.
- Fridge, heating and washing machine off. Phone on aeroplane mode.
- Avoid rush hour if you're near a main road, and close the windows.
- Record 10 seconds of silence at the start of each session. The editor uses it to clean up background hum.

## Recording the main talk (once)

1. Open the talk page, pick a scene, and read from the teleprompter panel. One scene at a time.
2. Fluff a line? Pause, then say the line again. The editor cuts it. Because you're small in the corner and the animation
   carries the screen, the cuts don't show.
3. Keep the energy up: you're talking to one parent on their sofa, not a hall.
4. Each scene's words are timed at 150 words a minute. Once you've recorded, the animation can be re-timed to your real
   pace from the transcript, so every pop and highlight lands on your words.

## Making the full frame intro look as good as the rest

Two options, both still genuinely you:

- **Plain:** the home set, as above. Clean and honest.
- **On brand:** most editors (Descript, CapCut, Canva) can cut you out of the background without a green screen.
  Put yourself on the paper coloured, moving world from the talk, with the sun and hills behind you. It looks like you're
  standing inside your own illustrations. Good light and a plain wall behind you make the cut out much cleaner.

## The "take a look at this" moment

The draft shows a video clip in the Paris scene. Olympic race footage belongs to the IOC and its broadcasters, so you need
permission to use it (ask British Rowing or Team GB). Simpler options that need no licence:

- **Hold the medal up to the camera.** "Take a look at this." Real, and better than footage.
- Your own photos or phone videos from Paris, training or the boathouse.

## Where else you could film

- **The Thames at Putney** is close to Clapham, and a boathouse would make a great intro background. It's echoey and busy,
  so use it only for short pieces, not the full talk.
- **A podcast studio hired by the hour** if you'd rather record the main talk in one go with proper lights and sound.
  Search "podcast studio hire London". You'd still film school intros at home, so match the framing.

## A filming day

1. Set up the corner, the light and the mic. Do a 30 second test and watch it back with headphones.
2. Record the main talk scene by scene (about an hour with retakes).
3. Record intros and outros for any schools already booked (5 minutes each).
4. Upload to the editor and send the files over.
