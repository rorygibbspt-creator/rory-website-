For parents

How to help your young athlete without adding pressure.

You're their lift to training, their kit washer and their biggest fan. Here's how to turn that support into the thing that helps them most.

Read the guide Mentoring for your child
The parents' guide

Five things that help

  • 1
    Protect sleep and routineScreens off time and calm evenings, rather than praising late nights or over packed schedules. Most young athletes perform better with around 8 to 10 hours.
  • 2
    Let the feelings land firstStraight after a match is rarely the time for feedback. Feel it first, then respond well. That goes for parents too.
  • 3
    Praise effort and characterNotice how they trained, how they treated teammates and how they handled a setback, not just the score.
  • 4
    Keep standards, protect the personThe strongest athletes care about standards and people. You can expect a lot and still be kind.
  • 5
    Play the long gameOne bad night, one bad game or one missed selection is not a disaster. What matters is the pattern.
